Chapter one, productivity. Your daily operating system. The productivity plugin is the general list of the collection. Where other plugins specialize in a domain, this one specializes in you. Helping you manage the daily flow of tasks, meetings, communications, and competing priorities that make up working life. Most people install this first. Many keep it permanently active alongside whatever domain specific plugins they use. It's the foundation layer. What IT does, the productivity plugin enhances Claude's ability to help you manage your work. Task management. Create. Organize. Track. And prioritize tasks using natural language. Tell Claude what you need to do and it structures it. Tell at what's urgent and it helps you sequence your day. Meeting preparation. Before a meeting, ask Claude to prepare a briefing. Pull in relevant context from your files, previous conversations, and connected calendar. Email triage. Face a wall of unread messages and need to know what actually matters. The productivity plugin applies prioritization frameworks to help you process your inbox efficiently, draft responses, and track follow-ups. Scheduling awareness. Connect your calendar and Claude becomes aware of your time, what's committed, what's free, and how to protect focused workblocks. Note synthesis. Consolidate information from across conversations, documents, and meetings into coherent summaries. This is in a replacement for your project management app or your calendar. It's the connective tissue that makes Claude more effective at helping you navigate your day. Getting started. The productivity plugin works immediately after installation. No external connections required. Install it from the plug-in sidebar and you're ready. For calendar awareness, you can connect your calendar through the plug-in settings. Co-work supports Google Calendar, Outlook, and Apple Calendar. The connection process follows the standard authorization flow. Click connect, sign in, grant access. Once connected, Claude can see your schedule and factor it into its recommendations. Calendar connection is optional. The plug-in is useful without it. Just less context aware about your time. Practical workflows. The morning briefing. This is the workflow that makes most productivity plug-in users permanent converts. First thing in the morning, open co-work and say something like, what's my day look like? I have a client call at 10 and need to finish the proposal by end of week. What should I prioritize? If your calendar is connected, Claude synthesizes your schedule with your stated priorities and any pending tasks from previous conversations. You get an actionable plan for the day, not just a to-do list. Meeting preparation. Before any significant meeting, ask Claude to prepare you. I have a call with a potential client tomorrow at 2pm. They're a marketing agency looking for design services. Prepare a briefing. What should I know and what should I bring up? Claude pulls context from any relevant files you've shared, previous conversations about this client and general industry knowledge to create a preparation document. The more context co-work has, through connected services and your accumulated interactions, the better these briefings become. Email triage. If you use co-works Gmail connector, you can process your inbox efficiently. I have about 40 unread emails. What needs a response today? What can wait? And what can I archive? What could categorize as by urgency? Flagged items that need action and can draft responses for the straightforward ones? You review the drafts, approve or edit, and move on. What would take 45 minutes of careful inbox processing becomes 10 minutes of review? Weekly review. At the end of each week, take stock. Let's do a weekly review. What did I commit to this week? What got done? What's carrying over? Claude reviews your recent conversations and task mentions, helping you close loops and plan ahead. This habit, just five minutes on Friday, prevents the slow accumulation of forgotten commitments that erodes trust with clients and colleagues. Capture without breaking flow. When something comes up mid-task, you don't have to switch contexts. Add to my tasks, follow up with Sarah about the website copy, due by Thursday. Claude logs it and returns to whatever you are working on. The task is captured. You haven't lost your train of thought. Tips. Tell Claude how you work. If you have a preferred productivity method, time blocking, a weekly review practice, a specific way you prioritize. Share it. The plugin adapts to your framework rather than imposing its own. I use the Eisenhower matrix for prioritization. Changes how Claude presents your options. Build context over time. The productivity plugin becomes more useful the more Claude knows about your recurring commitments, ongoing projects, and working patterns. You don't need to front load all of this. It accumulates naturally through use. Use global instructions. Co-works global instructions feature lets you set persistent context that applies to every session. Your role, your typical schedule, how you like information presented, set it once, and Claude remembers. This pairs powerfully with the productivity plugin.
