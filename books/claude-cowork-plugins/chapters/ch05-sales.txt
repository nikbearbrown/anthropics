Chapter 3. Sales. Finding and Winning Clients. Most people who need the sales plug-in don't think of themselves as sales people. The consultants, freelancers, agency owners, or builders who need clients but didn't go to school for pipeline management. The sales plug-in meets you where you are. It's not built exclusively for a 50% sales team with a sales force administrator, though it handles that too. It's useful the moment you need to research a prospect, prepare for a conversation, or follow-up after one. What ID does? Prospect research. Point cloud at a company or person and get a structured briefing. What they do, recent news, potential needs you can address, key contacts. This works from public information alone or becomes richer when connected to a CRM with relationship history. Call and meeting preparation. For any client conversation, get a briefing that includes relevant context, suggested talking points, and questions to ask. If you have previous notes or communications and co-worked successful folders, cloud weaves them in. Follow-up drafting. After a conversation, capture your notes and let cloud draft a personalized follow-up. Not a template, a message that references specific points from your discussion and proposes concrete next steps. Appline awareness. Even if your pipeline is a spreadsheet or a list in your head, the sales plugin helps you track where things stand. Which prospects need follow-up? Which conversations have stalled? What should you focus on this week to keep deals moving? Outreach. When you need to reach out to someone cold or warm, cloud drafts messages that are personal enough to feel human and structured enough to communicate value. Things started. Install the sales plugin from the plugin sidebar. It's useful immediately for prospect research and general sales support. Customization makes it significantly better. Tell cloud about. What you sell. Describe your services or products. Who therefore? And what problems they solve? Your sales process. Do you typically start with a discovery call? Do you send proposals? What does your path from first contact to close deal look like? Your ideal client. What characteristics make someone a great fit? What are the red flags that suggest they're not? If you use a CRM, even a simple one like HubSpot's free tier or a spreadsheet, you can connect it. CRM connection lets cloud reference your existing relationship data. Which makes research and preparation much richer. But it's not required. The plugin works with what you give it. Practical workflows. Pre-meeting research. You have a discovery call in an hour with a company you don't know well. I have a call with Meridian Design Studio at 2pm. They're a UX design agency. Research them. What do they do? Who are their clients? What's their recent news? And what might they need from someone like me? That produces a one-page briefing you can scan in five minutes. You walk into the conversation informed instead of improvising. This single workflow probably justifies the plugin for anyone who takes client meetings. Post-conversation follow-up. After the call, capture what happened while it's fresh. Just finish the call with Meridian. Key points. They're redesigning their internal tools. Timeline is Q2. Budget around $30,000. One concern is keeping existing users comfortable during transition. The decision-maker is their CTO, Lisa. Draft to follow-up email. Cloud drafts a personalized follow-up that hits each point. Suggests a logical next step. And maintains a professional but warm tone. You edit, add any personal touches. Adjust the tone to match your relationship. And send. What would normally sit on your to-do list for three days gets done in five minutes. Prospecting. When you need to find potential clients. I'm looking for UX agencies in the Pacific Northwest that might need front-end development support. What should I know about this market? And can you identify some companies worth reaching out to? Cloud researches the landscape and produces a list with context. Not just names, but what each company does and why they might be relevant. You then decide who's worth pursuing. Pipeline review. When you need to get a handle on where things stand. Here's my current prospect list. Paste or reference your tracking document. Which conversations need follow-up? What's gone cold? What should I focus on this week? Cloud reviews your pipeline and provides actionable recommendations. It's like having a sales manager who keeps you honest about follow-through. Without the awkwardness of actually having a sales manager. Outreach drafting. When you need to reach out cold. I want to reach out to the head of marketing at company. They recently posted about struggling with design resources. Draft and outreach message that's brief. References their situation and offers a conversation rather than a hard sell. Cloud produces something personal and concise. Not a template that screams automation. A message that demonstrates you've paid attention. Tips. Feed at your wins. When you close a deal, tell Cloud what worked, what resonated with the client, what objections came up, what sealed it. Over time, this builds institutional knowledge that improves future suggestions. Use it for the administrative burden. Activity logging. Note formatting. Follow-up scheduling. These are the tasks that sales professionals know they should do but skip when busy. Let the plug-and-handle them so you can focus on relationships. Automate relationship. The plug-and-handle is excellent at preparation and follow-through. It's not a replacement for genuine human connection during the actual conversation. Use it to show up prepared and follow up promptly but be fully present in the meeting itself. Keep your data current. Cloud can only work with what it knows. If your prospect notes are sparse or outdated, the plug-and-s briefings will be sparse too. The five minutes you spend capturing notes after each conversation, pay dividends in every future interaction with that prospect.
