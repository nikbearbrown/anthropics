Chapter 2. Installing and customizing plugins. The good news. Installing plugins is one of the simplest things you'll do in Co-Work. If you've ever installed an app from the App Store, you already have the required skills. Finding and installing plugins. Open Co-Work in the Claude Desktop app. In the left sidebar, you'll find a plugin section. Click into it and you'll see the library of available plugins and ThropX official collection plus any community or third-party plugins that have been added to the directory. Each plugin listing shows what it does, what domain it covers, and what capabilities it includes. Browse by function. Find the ones relevant to your work and click to install. That's it. The plugin downloads, activates, and is ready to use. You can also find plugins at Claude.com slash plugins, which provides the full directory with descriptions and documentation links. If someone has shared a custom plugin with you, a colleague's workflow, a community creation, something you've found online, you can upload it directly. Custom plugins come as files that you upload through the plugins interface. Click the upload option, select the file, and Co-Work handles the rest. Once a plugin is installed, its capabilities are available in your Co-Work sessions. Skills activate automatically when relevant. Slash commands appear in your command menu, type, or click plus. Connectors are ready to configure. Making a plugin yours. Here's where the real value emerges. Out of the box, plugins use sensible defaults, generic best practices for each domain. But you don't do generic work. You have your own processes, your own terminology, your own way of approaching problems. The customize feature lets you tailor any installed plugin to your specific situation. Find the plugin in your plugin sidebar, click customize, and Co-Work starts a guided conversation about how to make the plugin fit your work. This isn't a technical configuration process. It's a conversation. Co-Work asks you questions about your workflow, your preferences, and your tools. You answer in plain language. Based on your answers, the plugin adjusts its behavior. For the marketing plugin, customization might involve describing your brand voice, your target audience, and your preferred content formats. For the sales plugin, it might mean explaining your sales process, your qualifying criteria and what a good prospect looks like for your business. For the productivity plugin, it might be as simple as telling Co-Work about your meeting schedule and how you like your mornings organized. The level of customization is up to you. You can install a plugin and use it immediately with defaults. That's fine and still useful. Or you can spend 20 minutes customizing it and get something that feels like it was built specifically for your business. The choice depends on how central that plugins domain is to your work. What customization actually looks like? Since guided conversation can mean anything, here's what a real customization session looks like. Say you've installed the marketing plugin and clicked Customize. Claude asks, what does your business do and who are your primary customers? You say, I run a web design studio. My clients are mostly small professional services firms, accountants, lawyers, financial advisors who need modern websites but don't have in house marketing teams. Claude asks, how would you describe your brand voice? For example, are you formal and authoritative, casual and friendly, or something else? You say, professional but approachable. I want to sound like a knowledgeable friend, not a corporate agency. No jargon, direct. Claude asks, what content formats do you typically create? Blog posts, social media, email campaigns, something else? You say, mostly LinkedIn posts and a monthly email newsletter, occasionally a case study when I finish a big project. That's it. The conversation continues for a few more questions about your competitive landscape, your typical client journey and any terminology you use. But the pattern is always the same. Claude asks, you answer in plain language and the plugin calibrates. After this conversation, every piece of marketing content the plugin produces will be tuned to your business, the right audience, the right voice, the right formats. A content calendar suggestion will include LinkedIn posts and newsletter topics, not TikTok scripts and press releases. A competitive analysis will focus on other web design studios serving professional services firms, not enterprise agencies or DIY website builders. The difference between uncustomized and customized output is significant. It's the difference between getting generic marketing advice and getting advice from someone who understands your business. Connecting to your tools. Some plugins are self-contained. The productivity plugin works out of the box without connecting to anything else. Others become significantly more useful when connected to the tools you already use. The sales plugin, for instance, can research prospects from public information alone, but connected to your CRM and it can pull your existing relationship history, deal status and communication record into every interaction. The data plugin can work with files you provide, connect it to your database or data warehouse, and it can query your actual business data directly. Connections happen through the plugin's configuration. Co-work will guide you through connecting to supported services, which typically means authorizing access through the services standard login flow, similar to connecting any third party app. Not every plugin needs external connections to be useful. Start without them if you prefer and add connections later when you see the value. Managing your plugins. A few practical housekeeping notes. Enabling and disabling. If you want to temporarily turn off a plugin, perhaps you're focused on a specific project and want cloud to specialize. You can disable it without removing it. Your customization and configuration are preserved. Re-enable when you need it again. In-current. Plugins improve over time. Anthropic and community contributors add features, fix issues, and refine capabilities. Co-work handles updates, but it's worth checking occasionally whether your plugins have new features you might want to explore. Not overloading. Having many plugins installed doesn't slow co-work down or confuse cloud. But in practice, most people find that two or three active plugins cover their daily needs. You can always have others installed but disabled, ready to activate when you need them. Plugins are local. Your plugins, their configurations, and your customizations are stored on your computer. If you use co-work on multiple machines, you'll need to set up plugins on each one. Organization-wide plugin management, where an administrator can push plugins to a team, is coming, but not yet fully available. A quick test. After installing your first plugin, verify it's working with a simple test. If you installed the marketing plugin, try. What slash commands are available from the marketing plugin? Or simply ask co-work to do something in that plugin's domain. I need to plan content for next month. Can you help me build a content calendar? If the plugin is active, cloud's response will be noticeably more structured and domain aware than it would be without the plugin. It won't just give you generic content advice. It'll apply the marketing plugin's frameworks to produce something you can actually use. If something doesn't seem right, the plugin doesn't appear. Commands aren't recognized. Or results seem generic. Jump to the troubleshooting section in chapter 14. Most issues have simple fixes. You're now equipped to install and customize any co-work plugin.
