Chapter 6. Enterprise search. Finding what you already know. Ignore the name. Enterprise search sounds like something a Fortune 500 company deploys across 10,000 employees. But the problem it solves is embarrassingly universal. You wrote something down. You saved it somewhere. And now you can't find it. If you've been in business for more than a year, you have a knowledge retrieval problem. Client notes from 18 months ago that would be useful right now. Somewhere in Google Drive. The pricing rationale you worked out carefully and now can't locate. The process you documented for onboarding clients that's buried in a notion page, you haven't opened since you created it. The email where you agreed to specific terms with a vendor, which you need because they're now claiming something different. All of this information exists. You created it. You just can't get to it when you need it because file search is terrible. Your memory is fallible and the documents are scattered across three or four platforms. The Enterprise search plugin connects cloud to wherever your documents live and makes them searchable by content, not just by file name. You ask a question in plain language and cloud finds the answer in your own accumulated knowledge. What it does. Cross-platform search. If your files are scattered across Google Drive, Notion, Local Folders, and that one drop box account you keep meaning to organize, the Enterprise search plugin searches across all of them simultaneously. You don't need to remember where you put something. You just need to remember roughly what it was about. Content search, not just file name search. This is the critical distinction. Normal file search matches file names. If you name to document, Q3 notes, but it contains your decision about switching payment processors, searching for payment processor, won't find it. The Enterprise search plugin searches inside documents, matching your question against the actual content. Knowledge retrieval. Find that process you documented last year. Locate the decision you made about pricing and the reasoning behind it. Pull up the notes from that call with the client who's now asking the follow-up question. The plugin turns your document archives from a graveyard into a living resource. Context injection. When the Enterprise search plugin finds relevant documents, it can pull that information into your current conversation. Ask Claude about your pricing strategy, and it finds your pricing analysis document and uses it to inform its response. This means Claude's advice is grounded in your actual business context, not generic best practices. Getting started. Install the plugin and connect it to your document systems. The plugin supports Google Drive, Notion, Confluence, SharePoint, Dropbox, and Local File systems. This plugin does require connections to be useful. Its value comes entirely from searching your existing document repositories. Set up connections to wherever your important documents live. For most solar pranours, that's Google Drive and local files. If you also use Notion or Dropbox, connect those two. The initial indexing takes some time depending on how many documents you have. Once indexed, searches are fast. Practical workflows. Institutional memory. This is the core use case. When you need to remember why you made a decision. How did I decide to use Stripe instead of PayPal for processing? I made that decision last year, but I can't remember the reasoning. If you documented the decision, even casually, in meeting notes or a message to yourself, Claude finds it. This is invaluable when you're second-guessing a past decision, or when someone asks you to justify a choice you made months ago. Client context retrieval. A client you haven't spoken to in six months emails asking to resume a project. You need to remember where things stood. I need to resume work on the Henderson project. What do I have on file about it? Scope, timeline, last status, any open issues? Claude searches your connected repositories and assembles of briefing from whatever it finds. The original proposal, your project notes, the last email exchange, any status updates you wrote. In five minutes, you're up to speed on a project that would otherwise take an hour of digging through folders and email archives. Policy and process look up. When you need to check how you handle something, what's my standard cancellation policy for consulting engagements? If it's documented somewhere in your files, Claude finds it and gives you the answer. This works for any internal process or policy you've written down. Your invoicing terms, your project workflow, your client communication standards, preparation and enrichment. Before a meeting or call, pull everything you know about the topic. I have a call tomorrow with data viz, incorporated pull together everything I have on file about them. Proposals, emails, meeting notes, any work product. Claude assembles a comprehensive briefing from your own documents. Combined with the sales plug-ins external research, you walk into the conversation knowing both the public context and your private history. Avoiding contradictions. When you're about to write a proposal or set terms, check what you've said before. Have I ever quoted a price for website audit services? If so, what did I charge into whom? This prevents the awkward situation of quoting a different price to a new client than you charge someone in the same industry last year, whom might compare notes. Tips. The better your documents, the better the search. All-named files with clear content are easier for Claude to find and understand. This isn't a reason to reorganize everything now, but it's a reason to name things clearly going forward. Henderson Project Scope and Timeline is more searchable than document underscore final underscore v3. Specify where to look when you can. Search my notion workspace for the onboarding checklist is faster than searching everything. When you have a sense of where something lives, say so. Use it for synthesis, not just retrieval. What are the three approaches we've tried for client onboarding? Is more valuable than find the onboarding document. Claude can combine information across multiple documents into a coherent answer, which is something file search can never do. Build the habit of documenting decisions. The enterprise search plugin makes past documentation enormously valuable. Every time you make a decision and write down why, you're creating a searchable asset. A brief note, chose stripe over PayPal because of lower international fees and better API for future automation. Takes 30 seconds to write and might save you an hour of research a year from now.
