Introduction. Claude just got a team of specialists. When Claude Co-Work launched in January 2026, it gave you a capable generalist, an AI that could organize your files, create documents, process data, and manage the accumulated chaos of digital work. For many people, that was already transformative. Having a competent assistant who could actually do things, not just talk about them, changed what was possible in a working day. Claude and Anthropic released plugins, and the generalist became a team. Plugins transform Co-Work from a single capable assistant into a collection of specialists. Install the marketing plugin, and Claude understands campaign strategy, content calendars, and competitive positioning. Install the sales plugin, and it can research prospects, prepare call briefings, and draft personalized follow-ups. Install the legal plugin, and it can review a contract and flag the clauses you should worry about. There are 11 official plugins covering productivity, marketing, sales, research, data analysis, enterprise search, product management, customer support, legal, finance, and recruiting. Each one extends Claude's capabilities in a specific direction, teaching it the patterns and workflows of a particular domain. For a solopreneur or small team operator, this changes the math of what's possible. You've always known you should be doing competitive research, maintaining a content calendar, reviewing contracts carefully before signing them, and tracking your numbers properly. The problem was never awareness. It was bandwidth. There weren't enough hours, and hiring specialists for each function wasn't realistic. Plugins don't replace those specialists. They give you capable first pass coverage across functions you'd otherwise neglect, or handle poorly under time pressure. The marketing plugin won't replace the CMO, but it will help you produce a content brief that's better than what you'd scroll on the back of an envelope between meetings. The legal plugin won't replace outside council, but it will flag the concerning clauses in a vendor contract before you sign it without reading it, which is what you are probably going to do anyway. What this book does, this is a practical guide to making co-works plugins work for you. Each plugin chapter follows a consistent pattern. What it does, how to set it up, practical workflows you can use immediately, and tips for getting the most from it. By the time you finish the chapter's relevant to your work, you'll have installed and configured the plugins that matter for your role, learned the workflows that produce real results, customized plugin behavior for your specific situation, and understood what the plugins can and cannot do. This is a reference manual. You don't need to read every chapter. Jump to the domains that matter for your work. If you run your own marketing, read the marketing chapter. If you're building a product, read the product chapter. If you just want to get your daily workflow under control, start with productivity. What this book doesn't do. This book does not teach you co-work from scratch. If you're new to co-work entirely, if you haven't yet organized files or created documents with it, start with the Cloud Co-Work Handbook, which covers the foundations. This book does not require any coding knowledge. None. The setup process is straightforward. The customization is guided. And if you can install an app on your phone, you can install a co-work plugin. This book does not replace professional judgment. The legal plugin can flag contract risks, but it's not your lawyer. The finance plugin can model scenarios, but it's not your CFO. Every domain chapter includes appropriate caveats. Take them seriously. What you need. A Cloud subscription. Plugins require a paid plan. Pro. $20 per month. Max. $100 per month. Teams or enterprise. The free tier doesn't support plugins. Co-work access. Co-work is available on both Mac and Windows through the Cloud Desktop application. Download from Cloud.com slash download if you haven't already. Comfort with Co-work basics. You should know how to give Co-work a task, grant folder access, and review results. If these feel unfamiliar, spend time with the Cloud Co-work handbook first. Accounts for your connected tools. If you want the sales plugin to connect to your CRM, you need CRM access. If you want the data plugin to query your database, you need credentials. Plugins connect for your existing tools. They don't replace them. How to use this book. Part-I covers the foundation. What plugins actually are and how to install them. Read this even if you're eager to jump to your domain. It takes 10 minutes and saves confusion later. Part-2 is the reference section. One chapter per plugin domain, ordered by relevance to people running their own operations. Find your role. Read that chapter. Get set up. Most readers will engage deeply with two or three chapters. Part-3 covers advanced usage, combining plugins, troubleshooting, and building your own. A note on timing. Co-work is evolving rapidly. Plugins will gain features. Interfaces will change. New plugins will appear. Some details in this book will become outdated. The concepts won't. How to think about plugins. How to customize them for your needs. How to integrate them into your workflows. These patterns persist even as interfaces change. When something doesn't match what you see on screen, check the plugin's documentation page for current details. This book gives you the foundation. The official docs give you the latest specifics. Together, they keep you current.
