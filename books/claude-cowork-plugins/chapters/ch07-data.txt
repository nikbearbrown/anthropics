Data. Understanding your numbers. Here's something most sole apreneurs won't admit. They're afraid of their own data. Not in a dramatic way. More in the way that a spreadsheet of 12 months of revenue sits in a folder called finances and never gets opened because opening it means confronting numbers that need analysis. And analysis means formulas. And formulas mean three hours of Googling VillaCup syntax and still not trusting the answer. So the spreadsheet sits. And business decisions that should be informed by data get made on gut feel instead. The data plugin exists for this exact person. It lets you ask questions about your data in plain language, the way you'd ask a colleague, and get clear, accurate answers without writing a single formula. Data changes. Without the data plugin, here's what understanding your numbers looks like from most solo operators. You export a CSV from Stripe or QuickBooks or your invoicing tool. You open it in a spreadsheet. You stare at rows and columns. You may be sorked by date. You get a vague sense that things are going up or down. You close the file and go back to work. With the data plugin, you point Claude at that same file and say, which clients are generating the most revenue and has the mix changed over the past two quarters? Claude reads the data, performs the analysis, and gives you a clear answer in plain English. The entire interaction takes less time than it would take to write a sum formula. This is in a small shift. The gap between I think business is going okay. And I know that three clients account for 60% of my revenue, and one of them has been declining since September, is the gap between reacting to problems and seeing them coming. What he does? Natural language querying. Ask questions about your data in plain English. What were my total sales by month this year? Or which client brought in the most revenue in Q3? Or am I spending more on software subscriptions now than I was six months ago? Claude translates your question into the appropriate analysis and returns the answer, not as a formula or a table of raw numbers, but as a sentence you can understand and act on. Data exploration. Point Claude at a spreadsheet or database and ask it to help you understand what's in there. This is especially useful when you've exported data from a platform and received a CSV full of column headers that don't quite make sense. Claude describes the structure, identifies patterns, notes outliers, and most valuably suggests questions you might want to ask. Often the best question isn't the one you started with. Visualization. Turn data into charts. Show me a chart of monthly revenue for the past year. Produces of visualization you can use in presentations. Share with a business partner or simply look at to understand your trajectory. Claude chooses appropriate chart types. Line charts for trends. Bar charts for comparisons. Pie charts for composition. And you can adjust if you prefer something different. Data cleaning. Messy data is the norm, not the exception. Exported data arise with inconsistent data formats. Duplicate entries. Disgusting values. And mysterious columns that don't seem to contain anything useful. Before you can analyze data, you usually need to fix it. The data plug-in handles this tedious work and reports what it changed so you can verify before relying on the results. Comparison and benchmarking. How does this quarter compare to last quarter? Or is my client acquisition cost going up or down? These comparison questions are where data analysis earns its value. And where most people give up because building the comparison manually is tedious. Getting started. Install the data plug-in from the plug-in sidebar. The question you've been avoiding. Every business has one. The question you know you should answer but haven't because getting the answer felt too difficult. Maybe it's am I actually making money on my lowest-priced package? Or how much am I spending on tools I barely use? Start there. I have a spreadsheet of all my invoices from the past year and another of all my expenses. Am I profitable on a monthly basis? Which months were strongest and which were weakest? Are there any expense categories that are growing faster than revenue? Cloud reads both files, correlates them, and gives you a business health assessment. You'll know more about your financial position after this five-minute conversation than you did after a year of, I should really look at the numbers. Ad hoc business questions. When something comes up and you need an answer now, look at my sales spreadsheet and tell me which of my service packages has the highest margin. And has that changed over the past six months? Cloud reads the spreadsheet, performs the analysis, and gives you a clear answer. You didn't need to build a formula or remember how pivoted. Ables work. You asked a question and got an answer. Monthly business review. At the end of each month, point cloud at your financials. Here's my revenue and expense data for January. Give me a business health check. Total revenue, total expenses, profit margin, how it compares to December and anything that looks unusual. Cloud produces a concise review that highlights what matters. It's the kind of analysis you'd get from a fractional CFO done in minutes. Make this a habit, the first of the month, 10 minutes with the data plugin, and you'll never be surprised by your own numbers again. Data exploration. When you've exported data from a platform and need to understand it, I just exported my website analytics data. Walk me through what's in here and highlight anything interesting. Cloud examines the data structure, describes what it contains, and surfaces insights you might not have thought to look for. This is particularly useful when a platform gives you a data export with dozens of columns and no clear documentation of what they all mean. Cleaning and preparation. Before you can analyze data, it often needs fixing. This spreadsheet has inconsistent date formats, some duplicate rows, and a few entries where the amount is clearly wrong, negative values for sales. Clean it up and tell me what you fixed. Cloud handles the tedious cleanup work and reports what it changed, so you can verify the fixes before relying on the data. Data cleaning is the unglamerous prerequisite to useful analysis, and automating it removes the biggest barrier to actually working with your numbers. Client analysis. For service businesses, understanding your client base is critical. Realize my client data. Who are my top 10 clients by revenue? How concentrated is my revenue? What percentage comes from my top three? Are any long term clients spending less than they used to? Revenue concentration risk is one of the most important things a solar prerner can understand about their business. If one client represents 40% of your income, and you don't know that, you can't plan for the possibility of losing them. The data plugin makes this kind of analysis trivial. Tips. Start with questions, not requests. Analyze my data is vague and produces vague results. Why did revenue drop in March? Gives Cloud a specific question to answer. The more specific your question, the more useful the analysis. Keep your data files organized. If Cloud can find the right spreadsheet quickly, your analysis happens faster. A folder called Financial Data with consistently named files is easier to work with than scattered files across your hard drive. Itterate. Your first question often leads to a better second question. What were my top clients? Might lead to of those top clients, which ones have been declining. Which lead to what changed in Q3 that might explain the decline? Follow the thread. The data plugin makes follow up questions cheap, so ask them. Don't just look at totals. The most useful insights come from comparisons. This period versus last period. This client versus that client. This service versus that service. Totals tell you where you are. Comparisons tell you where you're heading. Export regularly. The data plugin can only analyze data you give it. Build a habit of exporting data from your key platforms, accounting software, payment processor, analytics on a regular schedule. A monthly export takes five minutes and gives you a clean data set for your monthly review.
