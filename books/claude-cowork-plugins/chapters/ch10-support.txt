Chapter 8. Support serving your customers. If you have customers, you have support needs. The support plugin helps you handle them efficiently, triaging requests, drafting responses, building knowledge bases, and identifying patterns in what people ask for. For solo operators handling their own support, this plugin turns a reactive burden into a manageable process. For small teams, it ensures consistency and speed across whoever's responding. What I does. Ticket triage. When requests come in, the plugin helps you categorize and prioritize them. What needs immediate attention? What can wait? What's a known issue with a standard answer? Response drafting. Generate replies that are helpful, on-brand, and appropriate to the situation. The plugin draws on your configured tone, your knowledge base, and the specific context of each request. Knowledge base building. Turn your accumulated support experience into a structured FAQ or help center. The plugin identifies common questions from your support history and drafts answers you can publish. Sentiment detection. Identify frustrated or at risk customers who need special attention. Not every support request is equal. The plugin helps you focus your personal attention where it matters most. Getting started. Install from the plugin's sidebar. For basic response drafting and knowledge base building, it works immediately. For richer integration, connect to your support platform, Zendesk, Intercom, FreshDesk, or Help Scout. You can also configure your tone. How do you want support responses to sound? Warm and friendly? Professional and efficient? Both? Escalation triggers. What keywords or situations should flag a request for your personal attention rather than a standard response? VIP identification. Which customers or domains deserve priority handling? Practical workflows. Morning triage. Start the day by processing what came in overnight. Here are the support messages from the last 12 hours. What's urgent? What's routine? And what can I answer with a quick response? Cloud categorizes the queue. Drafts responses for straightforward questions and flags anything that needs your personal attention. Your first 30 minutes of the day handles what might otherwise take two hours of context switching. Transparency assistance. For a specific tricky request, a customer is frustrated because their payment failed three times. They've been a client for two years. Draft a response that's empathetic, addresses the payment issue, and offers a concrete resolution. Cloud produces a response that matches your configured tone, acknowledges the customer's frustration, and proposes a path forward. You review, add any personal touch, and send. Draft a queue generation. When you're ready to build or update your help documentation. Based on the support requests I've handled this quarter, what are the most common questions? Draft FAQ answers for the top 10. Cloud identifies patterns in your support history and produces draft answers. You review for accuracy, add nuance where needed, and publish. Each FAQ entry reduces future support volume. Drafts. Draft. Don't autosend. Always review support responses before they go to customers. Cloud captures the right tone and content, but your personal judgment catches context the plugin might miss. Build your knowledge base progressively. Every time you handle a question that's likely to come up again, add it to your FAQ. Over time, this reduces your support burden significantly. Watch for patterns. The support plugin helps you see what customers repeatedly struggle with. These patterns are product feedback in disguise. Each recurring support request is a signal about what could be improved.
