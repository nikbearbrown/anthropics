# NARRATION — what-is-claude-research

## B00 (cold open)
Yassou, Bear. This channel runs on computational skepticism, and here's the trap it exists for: a wrong answer in a credible format is more dangerous than one that looks wrong, because it disarms the check. Citations arrive in a credible format. Here's how to use Claude for research without laundering a hallucination into your work.

## B01 (wrong model)
The wrong model: asking Claude for facts and pasting the answer. Citations in a credible format. You checked — it looked like a citation. It wasn't verified.

## B02 (right model)
The right model: ask for sources, read them yourself, use Claude to check your reading rather than replace it. Claude finds the thread; you pull it. The verification stays yours — that's the asymmetry.

## B03 (what's in the playlist)
Four moves: the difference between searching and recalling, verifying a citation back to its primary source, source laundering — a real number attached to the wrong report — and how to structure a literature review.

## B04 (do now)
Take the last Claude-assisted claim you made in public and find its primary source.

## B05 (verdict)
Who this is for: anyone whose work gets checked by someone else. If nobody ever checks your citations, this playlist will feel like paranoia — right up until someone does. Falsifiable line: if Claude's citations reliably point to sources that say what the citation claims, the verification step costs more than it saves.
