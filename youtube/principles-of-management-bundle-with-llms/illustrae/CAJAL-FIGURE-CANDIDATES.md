*Source: chapter 18 — "Stress and Well-Being"*

**PASTE:** Draw a blank 2×2 grid on a white background: four equal squares. A vertical axis arrow runs to the left (control dimension, low at bottom, high at top). No labels, no text, flat fills, uniform strokes.
- [S] single-column 89mm, 300 DPI, vector, white bg, square.
- [C] Karasek Demand-Control model: vertical axis = job control (low to high); horizontal axis = job demands (low to high). Four quadrants: High demand + Low control (bottom-right — high strain: ER nurses with rigid scripts, junior lawyers with no assignment input; highest health risk, strongest stress response); High demand + High control (top-right — active jobs: surgeons, skilled tradespeople, managers with genuine authority; challenging but not damaging); Low demand + High control (top-left — low strain: tenured academics, independent professionals; satisfying, not stressful); Low demand + Low control (bottom-left — passive jobs: security guards, rigid data-entry roles; dissatisfying but not acutely stressful). The mechanism: high demand you can control = challenge; high demand you cannot control = threat.
