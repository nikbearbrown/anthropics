# CARRY-OUT — GATE C

Written before any other narration. Every beat exists to make this sentence survivable.

## The line

> **A model being unsure which word comes next doesn't mean the words mean the same
> thing. Uphold and overturn are a coin flip too.**

## The wrong guess it defeats

That a watermark is safe where the model is undecided, because undecided means
interchangeable. It doesn't. Uncertainty is a fact about how probability is spread;
sameness of meaning is a fact about meaning. The second sentence carries the proof
inside the carry-out, which is why it earns its place.

## The secondhand test

*If someone repeats only this sentence in a meeting next week, is it still true?*

Yes — and the example travels with it. Someone who remembers nothing but "uphold and
overturn are a coin flip too" still has the whole argument.

## What it deliberately does not say

- Not "watermarks corrupt meaning." No evidence supports that as a general claim.
- Not "nobody checks meaning." Several papers do; the honest gap is narrower and S12 states it.
- Not a critique of anyone's evaluation. The reel grants what the studies measured
  and says what they measured instead.
