## Candidate 01 — Research the EGFR Resistance Playbook: T790M, Bypass Signaling, and Phenotypic Transformation
- Source: cancer-medicine/chapters/08-tumor-heterogeneity-and-clonal-evolution.md
- Lane: RESEARCH (Claude assistant)
- Hook: An EGFR inhibitor works beautifully for eleven months. Then the cancer grows back. The resistance mutation T790M was there at the start — too rare to detect — waiting while the drug killed every cell that could compete with it. The treatment created the conditions for its own failure. What are the routes out of this?

- Prompt seed: `claude "Research acquired resistance to osimertinib in EGFR-mutant non-small-cell lung cancer. Build a clinical decision tree for managing acquired resistance to osimertinib."`
- Read / check: confirm T790M is the classical first-gen resistance mechanism (not osimertinib); verify C797S as osimertinib resistance; confirm MET amplification frequency (~15-20%) and detection by FISH; confirm small-cell transformation frequency (~5%) and requirement for tissue biopsy; verify osimertinib plus savolitinib for MET-amplified resistance.
- Human supplies (Claude can't): Nothing — fully synthetic. All resistance mechanisms are published in NEJM and Journal of Thoracic Oncology.
- Output medium: Manim (animated clinical decision tree — EGFR inhibitor → treatment → resistance branch → detection → next therapy option)
- The change: ask Claude to compare whether pre-treatment ctDNA deep sequencing (looking for subclonal T790M or MET) could identify which patients will develop which resistance mechanism — what evidence exists for pre-resistance monitoring?
- Teardown angle: the treatment was a selection event. The drug did exactly what it was designed to do — killed sensitive cells — and in doing so, eliminated every competitor the resistant subclone had. The lesson is not to find better drugs; it is to sequence resistance before it happens and plan the sequence accordingly.
- Exclusions: full pharmacology of osimertinib, complete list of all EGFR mutations, ALK-rearranged lung cancer (a different molecular subtype).

## Candidate 02 — Research Chronic Inflammation and Cancer: Why a Wound That Never Heals Becomes a Tumor
- Source: cancer-medicine/chapters/06-the-tumor-microenvironment-signaling-inflammation-and-remodeling.md
- Lane: RESEARCH (Claude assistant)
- Hook: Rudolf Virchow saw immune cells in tumors in 1863 and proposed that cancer arises from chronic irritation. His colleagues ignored him. A hundred years later, the molecular biology proved him right — in detail. Chronic inflammation contributes to ~20% of all human cancer. And treating the inflammation prevents the cancer.
- The artifact: a sourced causal chain brief — four mechanisms linking chronic inflammation to carcinogenesis (DNA damage via ROS/RNS, proliferation pressure, pro-tumor cytokine signaling via IL-6/STAT3 and TNF-α/NF-κB, immune dysregulation toward tolerance) — with one clinical example per mechanism (H. pylori/gastric, IBD/colorectal, HBV/HCC, HPV/cervical) — animated as a Remotion four-node feedback cycle (inflammation → each mechanism → tumor → back to inflammation).
- Prompt seed: `claude "Research the four molecular mechanisms by which chronic inflammation promotes carcinogenesis: DNA damage from reactive oxygen/nitrogen species, proliferation pressure from repeated regeneration cycles, pro-tumor cytokine signaling (IL-6/STAT3 and TNF-α/NF-κB), and immune dysregulation toward tolerance. For each mechanism, provide one concrete clinical example where treating the inflammatory driver reduces cancer risk, with supporting evidence (H. pylori eradication, HBV vaccination, IBD management, or others)."`
- Read / check: verify H. pylori eradication reduces gastric cancer risk (published RCT evidence), confirm HCV cure with DAAs reduces hepatocellular carcinoma incidence, verify low-dose aspirin colorectal cancer reduction data and the bleeding risk trade-off that limits recommendation, confirm IL-6/STAT3 and TNF-α/NF-κB as the primary cytokine axes.
- Human supplies (Claude can't): Nothing — fully synthetic. All four mechanisms and their clinical validations are published.
- Output medium: Remotion (animated four-node feedback cycle — inflammation → damage/proliferation/cytokines/immune dysregulation → tumor → self-perpetuating)
- The change: ask Claude to construct the case for or against recommending long-term low-dose aspirin for colorectal cancer prevention in a 55-year-old with a family history — weighing the anti-inflammatory mechanism, the evidence, and the bleeding risk.
- Teardown angle: Virchow's 1863 observation is now a therapeutic principle. If the inflammatory process is the carcinogen, treating the process (eradicating H. pylori, curing hepatitis C, vaccinating against HBV) prevents the cancer. The same logic that explains cancer causation prescribes the intervention.
- Exclusions: full tumor microenvironment cellular biology, matrix remodeling details, immunotherapy mechanism.
- Score: 9/10

## Candidate 03 — Research the Cancer Stem Cell Debate: Hierarchy vs. Plasticity
- Source: cancer-medicine/chapters/07-cancer-stem-cells.md
- Lane: RESEARCH (Claude assistant)
- Hook: John Dick's 1997 AML experiment showed that only a tiny fraction of leukemia cells — CD34+CD38- cells — could reconstitute the cancer in a mouse. These were the cancer stem cells: the only cells that matter. Then Irving Weissman's lab did the same experiment with melanoma and found that almost every single melanoma cell could reconstitute cancer. The cancer stem cell model can't both be right and wrong. Who is correct?
- The artifact: a sourced synthesis brief resolving the hierarchy vs. plasticity debate — Dick 1997 (AML founding experiment), Quintana 2008 melanoma reversal, the immunodeficient mouse experimental artifact, and the current synthesis (hierarchical organization + phenotypic plasticity between states) — rendered as a Remotion timeline of key experiments + a two-panel comparison (hierarchy model vs. plasticity model vs. current synthesis).
- Prompt seed: `claude "Research the cancer stem cell hierarchy vs. plasticity debate. Explain Dick's 1997 AML founding experiment (CD34+CD38- cells reconstituting leukemia), then the Quintana 2008 melanoma challenge (high frequency of tumor-initiating cells in NSG mice vs. nude mice). What experimental artifact explains the discrepancy? What is the current synthesis — can cancer cells move bidirectionally between stem and non-stem states, and what regulates the transition? What are the therapeutic implications of each model?"`
- Read / check: confirm Dick 1997 AML paper (Bonnet and Dick, Nature Medicine), confirm Quintana 2008 melanoma NSG mouse finding (Morrison lab, Nature), verify the immunodeficient mouse assay artifact (xenograft conditions were too permissive — broader assay in NSG shows higher initiating frequency than SCID), confirm current understanding of EMT-driven stem cell plasticity.
- Human supplies (Claude can't): Nothing — fully synthetic. All landmark papers are published and citable.
- Output medium: Remotion (animated timeline of experiments + two-panel comparison: hierarchy model → Quintana challenge → current synthesis with bidirectional plasticity)
- The change: ask Claude to identify the therapeutic consequence of each model — if cancer stem cells are a fixed hierarchical minority, what treatment strategy follows? If phenotypic plasticity means any cell can de-differentiate, what strategy follows and why is it harder?
- Teardown angle: the debate revealed an experimental artifact — the immunodeficient mouse model was too permissive, artificially inflating the frequency of cancer-initiating cells. The resolution is not "one side was wrong" but "both sides were right in their specific experimental system." The biology itself (plasticity) makes the therapeutic problem harder than the hierarchy model implies.
- Exclusions: normal stem cell biology, hematopoietic stem cell transplant as treatment, CAR-T cell therapy.
- Score: 9/10

## Candidate 04 — Research Hot, Cold, and Excluded Tumors: Why Immunotherapy Works in Some Cancers and Fails in Others
- Source: cancer-medicine/chapters/10-cancer-immunotherapy-releasing-the-immune-system-on-cancer.md
- Lane: RESEARCH (Claude assistant)
- Hook: Pembrolizumab produces a 5-year survival of >50% in metastatic melanoma patients who previously had a life expectancy of months. In pancreatic cancer, checkpoint inhibitors show near-zero response rate. Same drug, same mechanism, completely different diseases. The answer lies in whether the tumor has T cells inside it or not.
- The artifact: a sourced classification brief — three tumor immune phenotypes (hot/inflamed, cold/desert, excluded/stroma-rich) with molecular definition, representative cancer types, checkpoint inhibitor response rates, and the strategies being tested to convert cold to hot — rendered as a Remotion three-panel animated comparison (each phenotype with immune cell density, characteristic cancer types, and intervention arrows).
- Prompt seed: `claude "Research the hot, cold, and excluded tumor immune phenotypes as predictors of checkpoint inhibitor response. For each phenotype: the molecular and histological definition, representative cancer types, checkpoint inhibitor response rates, and what strategies (TME modification, radiation, STING agonists, anti-stromal agents) are being tested to convert cold or excluded tumors to hot. Include data from landmark immunotherapy trials (KEYNOTE-006 melanoma, IMpower150 lung, pancreatic cancer failures)."`
- Read / check: confirm KEYNOTE-006 5-year melanoma survival >50%, verify the near-zero checkpoint response rate in microsatellite-stable pancreatic cancer, confirm that MSI-H status is the clearest biomarker of hot tumor (hypermutation → neoantigens), verify excluded phenotype in pancreatic cancer is driven by desmoplastic stroma (CAF-mediated exclusion).
- Human supplies (Claude can't): Nothing — fully synthetic. All immunotherapy trial data is published.
- Output medium: Remotion (animated three-panel comparison — hot / cold / excluded tumor phenotypes with immune cell density, cancer types, response rates, conversion strategies)
- The change: ask Claude to explain why tumor mutational burden (TMB) and PD-L1 expression are imperfect predictors of immunotherapy response and what additional biomarkers (gene expression signatures, spatial immune mapping) are under investigation.
- Teardown angle: the checkpoint inhibitor releases a brake. If there are no T cells in the tumor, releasing the brake on non-existent T cells does nothing. The biology of cold tumors is not a drug problem — it is an immune desert problem. Converting the desert to an inflamed tumor is the unsolved second act of immuno-oncology.
- Exclusions: CTLA-4 vs. PD-1 mechanism details (covered in Ch. 10), full list of checkpoint inhibitor approvals, CAR-T therapy.
- Score: 8/10

## Candidate 05 — Research the Tumor Microenvironment Puzzle in Pancreatic Cancer: Why Less Than 15% Are Cancer Cells
- Source: cancer-medicine/chapters/05-the-tumor-microenvironment-components.md
- Lane: RESEARCH (Claude assistant)
- Hook: A pancreatic cancer biopsy is stained and analyzed. Less than 15% of the cells are cancer. The rest are fibroblasts, immune cells, blood vessels, and dense extracellular matrix — the stroma. Drugs aimed at the cancer cells have to fight through all of this to reach their target. This is why pancreatic cancer has the worst 5-year survival rate of any major cancer. The real disease is the stroma.

Explain the cellular components (CAF subtypes: myCAF, iCAF, apCAF; TAM polarization; MDSC; CD8+ T cell and Treg balance), their functional roles (desmoplasia, immune exclusion, IFP elevation). Then survey the therapeutic strategies targeting the TME — anti-stromal agents, CAF reprogramming, CXCR4 inhibitors for immune cell trafficking — and explain why most have failed to improve survival in clinical trials despite mechanistic rationale."`
- Read / check: confirm pancreatic cancer stroma composition (<15% cancer cells, remainder stroma — published biopsy data), verify three CAF subtypes (myCAF/iCAF/apCAF — Bhatt 2021, Nature Cancer or similar), confirm CXCR4 inhibitor (AMD3100/plerixafor) rationale for immune cell trafficking, verify that hedgehog inhibitor stromal depletion (IPI-926) failed in PDAC and explain why (released the brake on stromal containment).
- Human supplies (Claude can't): Nothing — fully synthetic. PDAC TME composition is documented in multiple published studies.
- Output medium: Remotion (animated stacked composition chart — TME cell types and proportions — + second panel: therapeutic strategies and their trial outcomes)
- The change: ask Claude to explain the PEGPH20 hyaluronidase failure — why the mechanistically sound strategy of reducing interstitial fluid pressure did not improve survival — using the "TME as network" principle from the chapter.
- Teardown angle: the stroma is not just an obstacle to drug delivery — it is also a containment mechanism. Some stromal components actively suppress tumor invasion. Stripping the stroma (as hedgehog and hyaluronidase strategies did) removed the drug delivery barrier AND the cancer containment barrier simultaneously. Network interventions require understanding all edges, not just the one being targeted.
- Exclusions: nanoparticle drug delivery to PDAC, FOLFIRINOX regimen details, genetic drivers of PDAC (KRAS G12D).
- Score: 8/10

## Candidate 06 — Research Cancer Immunoediting: Elimination, Equilibrium, and Escape
- Source: cancer-medicine/chapters/09-tumor-immunology-surveillance-antigens-and-the-immune-response.md
- Lane: RESEARCH (Claude assistant)
- Hook: In 2001, Robert Schreiber's lab showed that immunodeficient mice developed more cancers more quickly than immunocompetent mice — proving that the immune system is not just a passenger in cancer development. It is actively suppressing tumor growth, and cancer evolves to escape. The cancer you see in the clinic is the one that won the selection.
- The artifact: a sourced three-phase brief on cancer immunoediting — elimination (immune surveillance clearing pre-cancerous cells), equilibrium (dormancy under immune pressure — the longest and most clinically invisible phase), and escape (immune evasion enabling clinical cancer) — with the molecular evidence for each phase and the clinical implication — animated as a Manim three-phase timeline (immune pressure → equilibrium dormancy → escape clones emerge → clinical cancer).
- Prompt seed: `claude "Research the three phases of cancer immunoediting (elimination, equilibrium, escape). For each phase: explain the molecular mechanism, what experimental evidence supports it (key mouse model experiments and clinical observations for elimination; the xenotransplant dormancy evidence for equilibrium; MHC downregulation, PD-L1 upregulation, antigen loss for escape). Then explain how understanding escape mechanisms led directly to the design of checkpoint inhibitors."`
- Read / check: verify Schreiber 2001/2002 immunoediting papers (Science — Shankaran et al.), confirm the immunoequilibrium evidence (transferred tumors regrow faster in immunocompromised hosts), verify that tumor-infiltrating lymphocyte presence (TIL density) predicts survival before checkpoint inhibitors, confirm MHC class I downregulation as the most common escape mechanism.
- Human supplies (Claude can't): Nothing — fully synthetic. Immunoediting framework is published and the founding papers are citable.
- Output medium: Manim (animated three-phase timeline — elimination → equilibrium → escape → clinical cancer, with molecular events at each phase labeled)
- The change: ask Claude to identify the equilibrium phase as the most therapeutically interesting — could drugs that maintain immune equilibrium (preventing escape) be a cancer prevention strategy, and what evidence exists for this concept?
- Teardown angle: the immunoediting framework explains why the cancer you see is always the one that already evaded detection. The tumor in the clinic is not a random sample of all possible cancers — it is the survivor of selection by the immune system. Understanding this changes how you think about early detection and prevention.
- Exclusions: complete checkpoint inhibitor clinical trial data, CAR-T therapy, tumor antigen classification in full detail.
- Score: 8/10
