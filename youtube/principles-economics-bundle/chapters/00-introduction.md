## A Note about AI

Economics is the field where the model is most fluently model-rich and most casual about the empirical question of which model applies. The note examines how to use the model without inheriting its smoothness about contested questions.

The model has read every introductory economics text — micro and macro. It will produce competent paragraphs on supply and demand, elasticity, market structures, consumer theory, the major macroeconomic schools, monetary policy, fiscal policy, growth theory, and the canonical international trade arguments. The fluency is real and useful for vocabulary and orientation.

Where the model genuinely helps: explaining a model (what consumer surplus is, what a Nash equilibrium implies, what the Phillips curve was and what its instability taught the field, what the Mundell-Fleming framework predicts), walking through the standard worked examples in microeconomics, surveying the historical development of macroeconomic thought with attention to what each school was a reaction against, and producing structured comparisons across models.

Where the model does damage: characterizing current macroeconomic conditions, recommending policy, declaring contested empirical questions settled, and treating model predictions as predictions about the world rather than about a stylized representation of it. The model has read enormous quantities of economic commentary, much of it partisan in ways the model has absorbed. Its descriptions of current policy debates can carry inflection invisible from the prose.

A specific failure mode worth naming: the model is particularly fluent in producing policy recommendations that sound principled and rest on assumptions the model is willing to make and the analyst should not. Asked whether a specific policy would be welfare-enhancing, the model will produce an answer; the answer depends on assumptions about elasticities, distributional weights, and counterfactual scenarios that should be made explicit and rarely are.

The rule that covers all three: models from the model; the policy question from the analyst's explicit assumptions and the data. Economics is taught as a discipline of careful conditional reasoning — *if* these assumptions, *then* this conclusion. The model is fluent in the conclusions and casual about the assumptions. Read its policy prose against the *if* and see whether you accept it.
