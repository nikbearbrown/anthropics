## A Note about AI

University physics covers the same content as college physics with greater mathematical depth — calculus-based mechanics, electromagnetism, thermodynamics, waves, and the foundations of modern physics. The model's strengths and weaknesses are the same as in any quantitative subject: conceptual fluency, computational unreliability. The note examines what that means at the calculus-based level.

The model has read every standard university physics text (Halliday-Resnick, Young-Freedman, Sears-Zemansky, Tipler). It explains principles, walks through derivations, translates between integral and differential forms of the same law, and produces canonical worked examples. The conceptual fluency is real and useful — university physics demands that students hold the same principle in multiple representations, and the model is good at producing alternative representations.

Where the model genuinely helps: explaining the conceptual content of calculus-based mechanics (what Newton's second law as a differential equation buys you, why the work-energy theorem follows from F=ma, what the kinematic equations are integrating), walking through derivations with attention to which step is the physics move and which step is the mathematical move, producing alternative formulations of the same principle (Lagrangian vs. Newtonian formulations of mechanics, integral vs. differential forms of Maxwell's equations), and surfacing common misconceptions before they form.

Where the model does damage: performing the actual integration, the actual vector decomposition, the actual boundary-value-problem solution. The model is fluent in symbolic manipulation and unreliable as a calculator at the precision university physics requires. It will drop signs, miss limits of integration, mishandle vector components, and confidently report numerical answers off by factors that any consistency check would catch.

A specific failure mode worth naming: the model is particularly prone to errors in problems requiring careful attention to direction conventions — sign of gravity, direction of normal vectors in electromagnetism, sign conventions in optics. The errors are textbook-typical (the exact errors students make) which means model output cannot be used to check student work in a meaningful way.

The rule that covers all three: principles and derivations from the model; the calculation from your pen, with consistency checks at every dimensional and limit-case boundary. University physics is taught through problem-solving at a level of precision the model is not reliable at. Use the model to understand. Do the work yourself.
