## A Note about AI

Physics with the model in the loop is a useful tutor on principles and an unreliable computer of specifics. The textbook teaches both. The note addresses where the model belongs in each.

The model has read every introductory physics text. It explains Newton's laws, conservation principles, the laws of thermodynamics, waves and oscillations, electromagnetism, and the foundations of modern physics. It produces canonical derivations on request and translates between different framings of the same principle (Newtonian, Lagrangian, energy-based) without difficulty. This conceptual fluency is real teaching value — physics is a field where the same principle understood in three different ways is more solid than the same principle memorized once.

Where the model genuinely helps: explaining what a physical principle is doing (what conservation of momentum implies for the canonical collision problem, what entropy is and is not, what a field is), walking through the structure of a derivation with attention to which step is the physical move and which is the mathematical move, producing worked example problems at graded difficulty, surfacing common misconceptions before they form, and producing alternative analogies for confusing concepts (relativity-of-simultaneity through trains and lightning, the wave function through extended-membrane analogies).

Where the model does damage: performing the actual calculation for a specific problem. The model is unreliable as a calculator and confident as one. It will mix unit systems, drop signs in vector decompositions, fumble factors in derived expressions, and present orders-of-magnitude-wrong numerical answers without flagging the wrongness. The errors cluster around exactly the steps where students typically err, which means the model is not a useful proofreader for your work.

A specific failure mode worth naming: the model is fluent in symbolic manipulation of formulas and casual about the physical meaning of the symbols. It will combine equations that share variable names but describe distinct physical quantities. It will treat scalar and vector versions of an equation as interchangeable. It will use inconsistent sign conventions across closely related problems. A student copying model work imports these inconsistencies invisibly.

The rule that covers all three: principles and derivations from the model; the actual calculation and the numerical answer from your work. Physics is taught through the discipline of getting the answer right — units, signs, magnitude — and that discipline is built by doing the calculations yourself. The model can show you what force is. It cannot draw your free-body diagram in a way you should trust without checking.
