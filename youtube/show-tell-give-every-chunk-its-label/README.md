# Give Every Chunk Its Label

**What this is.** A show-tell explainer (about 2.6 minutes) of contextual retrieval, from Anthropic's cookbook notebook `claude-cookbooks/capabilities/contextual-embeddings/guide.ipynb`. It is drawn as one cast. A long white document is cut into four chunk cards, and one card, cut loose, has lost its context, so a search lens glides past it. Claude, a kraft block with a terracotta spark, reads the whole document plus the chunk and writes a short kraft note that sits on the front of the card. Card and note are embedded together into an index. Two bars show the notebook's headline: contextual embeddings cut the top-20-chunk retrieval failure rate by 35%, averaged across all data sources Anthropic tested (per Anthropic's notebook). A cache shelf holds the document so every later chunk reads it back at a 90% discount. A BM25 keyword board sits beside the embedding index, and their two rankings merge into one list. A reranker scan line sorts a longer column, and the best few go back to Claude. Labels only; Liam's voice does the explaining. **Why.** Card #16 in `../show-tell-ideas.md` (Batch 2), built on Bear's overnight order of 2026-09-27. **Found.** Every spoken claim matches the notebook. The film uses only two of its numbers: the 35% headline, quoted with its exact framing and attributed aloud and on screen, and the 90% cache-read discount, attributed aloud. The notebook's other figures are left out because some contradict each other (its intro credits contextual embeddings with Pass@10 "~87% --> ~95%", but its own table reaches 95% only with reranking). In the notebook the reranker reads the embedding results, not the hybrid list, so the film never says which list feeds it.
