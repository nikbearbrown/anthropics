# Fact check

- A deterministic universal perfect cloner cannot be unitary because input overlap `z` would have to equal output overlap `z^2` for every pair.
- `z=0` corresponds to orthogonal states; `z=1` corresponds to the identical normalized ray under the proof's phase convention.
- A basis-specific unitary can copy a known orthogonal set; CNOT copies computational-basis states into a blank target.
- Known states can be independently prepared. Approximate and probabilistic cloning do not contradict the theorem.
- Perfect discrimination of arbitrary nonorthogonal states from one specimen is impossible.

The script excludes QKD, error correction, Kraus operators, and no-signaling detours as required by the candidate card.
