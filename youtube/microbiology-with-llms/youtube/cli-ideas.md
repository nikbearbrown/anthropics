The video shows Claude producing the first draft, the researcher challenging one entry, and Claude revising with a correction.
- Prompt seed: `claude "I will give you a clinical vignette. Generate a ranked differential diagnosis as a table: Organism | Gram stain | Primary disease | Key distinguishing feature. Do NOT include drug doses. Flag any entry where you are less confident and explain why. Vignette: 45-year-old with fever, severe headache, neck stiffness, CSF shows Gram-positive diplococci in pairs, glucose low. Rank the top 5 organisms."`
- Read / check: Verify the top entry (Streptococcus pneumoniae, Gram-positive diplococci) against Harrison's Principles or UpToDate; check that Claude does not invent drug doses; confirm that the correction exchange (challenge and revision) is authentic — do not script the revision.

- Teardown angle: Claude is a sparring partner for clinical reasoning, not an oracle. The value is in the follow-up, not the first reply — the correction exchange is where reasoning happens. Confident first-draft errors are more useful than no answer.
- Exclusions: Drug dosing (explicitly excluded per chapter guidance); antibiogram local data; treatment algorithms.

- Prompt seed: `claude "Write Python Manim: plot two bacterial growth curves on a log₁₀ scale y-axis, time axis 0-72 hours. Curve 1: E. coli, lag phase 0-1h, exponential N=2^(t/0.333) until 10^9/mL, stationary 10-24h, death 24-72h. Curve 2: M. tuberculosis, same phases scaled for g=18h. Label the four phases with sweep-in annotations. Second panel: animate the numeric readout of N=1×2^(720/20) at t=12h for each organism. Vox palette."`
- Read / check: Verify 2^36 ≈ 6.87×10^10 (close to 7×10^10 as stated); check that M. tuberculosis at t=12h gives 2^(12/18) ≈ 1.59; confirm the stationary plateau is at 10^9 for E. coli per chapter text; verify log scaling is correct.
- Human supplies: Nothing — fully synthetic. The growth parameters are from the chapter (20-min E. coli, 18-hr TB, 10^9 stationary cap).
- Output medium: Manim (animated)
- The change: Add a third curve — a hypothetical organism with g=10 min (the fastest documented growth) — and animate the "if exponential continued for 48 hours = Earth mass" endpoint as an absurdity marker that makes the stationary-phase mechanism visceral.
- Teardown angle: The growth curve is the single most predictive tool in microbiology — every clinical timeline (infection progression, culture turnaround, antibiotic response window) is a consequence of the organism's generation time. - Score: 9/10

## Candidate 03 — "Research Antibiotic Resistance Mechanisms with Claude: The Four Ways Bugs Fight Back"
- Source: microbiology-with-llms/chapters/14-antimicrobial-drugs.md
- Lane: RESEARCH (Claude assistant)
- Hook: Every antibiotic class has a counter-mechanism. MRSA blocks beta-lactam binding. Fluoroquinolone-resistant E. coli mutates the target. Gram-negatives pump efflux pumps that export the drug before it reaches concentration. Claude can synthesize all four in a sourced brief — but will confabulate specific resistance rates if unchallenged.
- The artifact: A sourced 4-row resistance-mechanism brief: Mechanism | Example organism/drug pair | Genetic basis | Clinical consequence — covering (1) target modification, (2) enzymatic inactivation (beta-lactamases/ESBLs), (3) efflux pumps, (4) reduced permeability. Generated by Claude, then challenged for one specific resistance rate that must be verified against a local antibiogram disclaimer.
- Prompt seed: `claude "Produce a sourced table of the four primary antibiotic resistance mechanisms: target modification, enzymatic inactivation, efflux pumps, and reduced permeability. For each: give an organism/drug pair example, the genetic basis (plasmid vs. chromosomal), and the primary clinical consequence. Do NOT give specific resistance rates — note that these are local and must be checked against a current antibiogram. Flag any mechanistic claim you cannot verify with a primary source."`
- Read / check: Verify that mecA gene encodes PBP2a in MRSA for target modification; confirm that ESBL-producing organisms are plasmid-mediated; check that efflux pumps are correctly attributed to Gram-negatives (AcrAB-TolC in E. coli); ensure no resistance rate figures appear (explicitly excluded per the prompt).
- Human supplies: A current IDSA or WHO resistance surveillance report for the verification beat; screen-recording of Claude generating and revising the table.
- Output medium: screen-recording mp4
- The change: Challenge Claude on one mechanism with: "The chapter says Gram-negative outer membrane reduces permeability — but you can have a Gram-negative with an efflux pump AND reduced permeability AND an ESBL. Does combining mechanisms multiply resistance? What does the literature say?" and capture the synthesis.
- Teardown angle: Resistance mechanisms are not competing explanations — they stack. An organism can have three or four simultaneously, which is why pan-resistant organisms are so dangerous. Claude's strength here is synthesis across mechanisms; its weakness is confident specificity on local resistance rates it cannot know.
- Exclusions: Drug dosing and regimens (explicitly excluded); specific resistance percentages; pharmacokinetics/pharmacodynamics detail.
- Score: 8/10
