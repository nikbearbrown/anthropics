# VISUAL GRAMMAR — the descent

**Reel:** `claude-liam-four-places-your-data-goes` · "Three You Can Take Back. One You Can't."
**Supersedes** the "recurring visual" section of `PLAN.md`.

The four uses are staged as a **descent through ordered, named places, each narrower than the last, with a threshold at every level.** The structure is Dante's; nothing in the reel says so.

**Silence is the rule.** No inscription, no Italian, no "abandon hope," no circles numbered in Roman, no Virgil, no mention of Dante in narration, on screen, in the title, or in the description. The grammar works exactly as long as nobody names it — same discipline as the `Sawubona` greeting. A viewer who knows the *Inferno* feels the shape; a viewer who doesn't sees a clear diagram of where their data goes. Both are served. **If a build note ever proposes surfacing the reference, the answer is no.**

---

## Who is who

The distinction the whole grammar rests on, and the one a builder will get wrong if it isn't stated:

- **The viewer is the pilgrim.** Passing through, looking, leaving. Dante walks out the bottom.
- **The detail is the soul.** `partner · hiking · near Denver` descends and, at the fourth level, stays.

This is why the grammar survives the objection that the *Inferno* ends in escape. The pilgrim escapes. The residents don't. Never animate the viewer's POV descending *with* the detail past level 4 — the camera watches the detail go where the camera does not follow.

## The five structural borrowings

**1 · Descent, not a list.** The four uses never appear as four boxes side by side. They stack downward and the reel only ever moves down. One rung per act.

**2 · Narrowing.** Each level is materially narrower than the one above it — the inverted cone. This is not decoration, it is true: the conversation holds everything you typed; memory holds a selected subset; provider systems hold logs and a retention window; training holds one adjusted pattern. **Each stage keeps less, and keeps it longer.** The taper carries that without a word.

**3 · A threshold at every level.** Each rung has a doorway glyph.

| Level | Glyph | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · conversation | doorway, double-headed arrow through it | walk in, walk out |
| 2 · product memory | doorway, double-headed arrow, a latch | walk out, but somebody set the latch |
| 3 · provider systems | doorway, double-headed arrow, a clock face on the frame | you can leave; you don't set the clock |
| 4 · training | **arch, single arrow, no return path drawn** | the only one-way shape in the reel |

The rung-4 arch is **the single repeating terracotta in the episode.** Everything else is warm ink. Per accent law, no other element in that beat may be terracotta.

**4 · Named places.** Each level carries its name in Title Case serif on the rung as it's reached. `The Conversation` · `Product Memory` · `Provider Systems` · `Training`. The names persist in the compressed rail above (see canvas solve).

**5 · The guide who narrates but never appears.** Liam is Virgil. Nothing to build — just don't put a figure on screen.

## The camera law

**The camera never pans up.** Each act's establishing frame begins where the previous act's ended and continues downward. There is exactly one upward move in the reel: **B20**, the pull-back that reveals all four levels at once. That is the pilgrim stepping out and looking back at the structure — it lands harder because it is the only time the frame reverses direction. Do not spend the move earlier.

## Palette discipline — the brand law that constrains this

CLAUDE is a **fidelity** brand. Cream `#F2F0E9` stage, warm ink `#3D3929`, one terracotta `#D97757`. **A Dante grammar must not reach for infernal colour** — no reds, no darkening gradient, no vignette deepening per level, no shift toward black at the bottom. Retinting the stage to signal depth breaks brand law and is the most likely way this idea goes wrong in the build.

Descent is signalled by **layout and weight only:**

- vertical position — each act sits lower in the safe area
- width — the taper
- rule weight — hairlines thicken by one step per level (deeper = heavier line, never darker page)
- the spark line descends with the level, top-anchored at level 1, bottom-anchored by level 4

## The canvas solve (FILL-THE-CANVAS LAW)

A descent through the frame invites dead space at the top, which is a defect under FILL-THE-CANVAS. The solve:

**The active rung fills the safe area.** Levels already passed compress into a **rail** across the top of the frame — thin stacked bands, each carrying its name and its resolved gate glyph at small size. The rail grows by one band per act; the active rung always occupies the remaining space and is sized to fill it. By level 4 the rail holds three bands and the arch owns the rest of the frame at full scale.

The rail doubles as a progress indicator and guarantees the frame is never empty above the action.

## Rights

*Divina Commedia*, c. 1308–1321. Public domain, no estate, no mark. **No translation is quoted**, so no translation's copyright is engaged. If a future cut ever wants a line — it shouldn't — Longfellow (1867) and the original Italian are safe; Ciardi, Musa, Hollander, Pinsky and the modern translations are not.

## The test

Show the reel to someone who has read the *Inferno* and someone who hasn't. If the first says "that was a nice diagram" and the second says "wait, is that Dante?", the grammar failed in both directions. Correct outcome: the second understands where their data goes; the first also feels the descent and never has it explained to them.
