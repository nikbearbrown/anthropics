## B00 (Cold Open)
Bonjour, Bear. This channel runs on computational skepticism, and Popper's rule applies to your prompts too: a claim that can't be wrong isn't a claim yet. If you can't say what a bad answer would look like, you haven't written a spec. Here's why 'be more specific' doesn't actually help.

## B01 (Wrong model)
The wrong model: prompting as spellcasting. Collecting phrases like 'you are an expert' and hoping one lands. Adjectives stacked on adjectives. Nothing specific about what done looks like.

## B02 (Right model)
The right model: prompting as writing a brief. Audience, format, constraints, what done looks like, one example. You write the spec before you see the output — that's the Popperian move.

## B03 (Playlist contents)
The playlist covers four moves: why examples beat adjectives, naming the failure you want avoided, asking for the reasoning, and iterating the spec rather than just the wording.

## B04 (Do now)
Tonight: take your worst recent output and add the one requirement you never said out loud. Not the wording — the missing constraint.

## B05 (Verdict)
Who this is for: people who've read the prompt-hack threads and got nothing out of them. Skip it if you already write briefs for humans — same skill, different medium. Falsifiable line: if adding an explicit example and a named failure case to a prompt produces no measurable difference in output quality, the spec model is wrong.
