## Source document

- Bear's research prompt, *"How to Tell When a Change Helps vs. Just Produces a
  Different Kind of Wrong"* — a first-principles methodology doc supplied in the
  Cowork session. Structure: a core-problem frame; Parts 1–6 (define "helped",
  signal-vs-noise, error-taxonomy shape, evaluator overfitting, symptom-vs-cause,
  longitudinal regression suites); Part 7 (literature grounding); a standalone
  evaluation prompt; a self-flagged synthetic appendix; and a Reference Lineage.
- The doc is the episode's spine. It is NOT a published paper — it is Bear's own
  synthesis, and it explicitly annotates which of its parts are verified vs
  invented (see FACTCHECK.md "EXCLUDED by design").

## Reference lineage cited on screen (B31 + sources card) — each re-verified at Gate F

- Goodhart's Law (measure-becomes-target critique, ~1975).
- CheckList — behavioral testing of NLP models across capability categories.
- Dynabench — dynamic, human-and-model-in-the-loop benchmark creation.
- HELM — Holistic Evaluation of Language Models (multi-metric reporting).
- McNemar's test — paired binary before/after outcomes on the same items; NIST
  reference + the small-sample exact-binomial caveat.
- PoLL — Panel of LLM evaluators (a panel reduces single-model judge bias).
- Anthropic & OpenAI prompt-engineering / eval guidance (not every failing eval
  is a prompt fix; LLM judges discriminate better pairwise than open-ended).

## Corrections / cautions

- The source mixes verified method with an admittedly-invented metric
  ("Exponential Impact Score") and a synthetic worked example (p ≈ 0.39, the
  40-case walkthrough). Both are EXCLUDED from the episode per FACTCHECK.md —
  the reel teaches the method, never the invented numbers. This caution is itself
  on-theme: the episode is about not inheriting a source's confidence.
