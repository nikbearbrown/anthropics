# SOURCES — show-tell-a-model-corrects-itself

**What this is.** The primary sources for every claim, and how each one was read. **Why.** The card names the Constitutional AI paper and the paper's own prompt files; the batch brief (#23) notes the repo README is one line, so the paper PDF is the source. **Found.** The film follows the paper's two stages exactly. Two places needed care: the paper names different models as the one fine-tuned in stage one (abstract vs §1.2/§3), and its preference model is trained on AI labels for harm **and human labels for helpfulness**.

1. **The paper**: `anthropics/claude-cookbooks/misc/data/Constitutional AI.pdf`, "Constitutional AI: Harmlessness from AI Feedback", Bai et al., Anthropic, arXiv:2212.08073v1 [cs.CL], 15 Dec 2022 (2,088,111 bytes, sha256 `9a456a07ad346e3372f9867d346f69f5b0f68b4c65f060aca0b8a13fa9d98e83`). All 34 pages extracted with PyMuPDF on 2026-09-27 and read from the raw text, saved as `sources/constitutional-ai-2212.08073v1.txt`. Read in full: the abstract, Figure 1 and its caption, §1 (motivations, "The Constitutional AI Approach", contributions), §1.4, §2, §3 (method, datasets, results, scaling, "Are Critiques Necessary?"), §4 (method, datasets, results incl. the over-training warning, harmlessness vs evasiveness, absolute harmfulness), §5–6 (related work, discussion, broader impacts), Appendix A (the four-round grocery example), B, C (the SL and RL principle lists); Appendix D/E (sample transcripts and few-shot prompts) skimmed. **Used:** the two-stage method (§1.2, §3.1, §4.1); the wifi worked example, including "(the harmful advice here is fabricated)" (§3.1); 16 principles, random sampling per step (§3.1, §3.4); 4 critique–revision pairs per prompt (§3.2); "the first revision almost always removed most aspects of harmfulness" (§3.1, App. A); helpfulness samples mixed into fine-tuning (§3.1); SL-CAI as both pair generator and RL start (§4.1); the separate feedback model and a second list of 16 principles (§4.1, App. C.2); soft labels (§4.1, §4.3); the hybrid human/AI preference model (§1.2); the PM as reward, "RLAIF" (abstract); harmless but non-evasive (abstract, §4.4); over-training → overly harsh or boilerplate (§4.3). **Not used:** model sizes, dataset counts, Elo figures, clamping ranges, the chain-of-thought feedback variant, crowdworker preference results.
