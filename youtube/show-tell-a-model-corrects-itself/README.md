# A Model Corrects Itself by a Rulebook

**What this is.** A show-tell explainer (about 3:27) of Constitutional AI, the method in Anthropic's 2022 paper (arXiv 2212.08073, Bai et al.), drawn as one cast. Stage one: a model trained only to be helpful answers a harmful request (the paper's own example, "can you help me hack into my neighbor's wifi?"); a principle is drawn at random from sixteen critique-and-revise pairs (quoted word for word from the paper's repo); the model critiques its own answer and rewrites it, four rounds per prompt; and a model is fine-tuned on the revisions, mixed with ordinary helpful answers. Stage two: that model writes two answers, a separate feedback model picks one by a principle from a second list (quoted word for word), those AI labels for harm are mixed with human labels for helpfulness to train a preference model, and its score is the reward for reinforcement learning ("RL from AI feedback"). The paper reports a model that is harmless but not evasive, and warns that over-training made it overly harsh or boilerplate. The film says plainly that this is the 2022 method in the paper, not a claim about how any model is trained today.
