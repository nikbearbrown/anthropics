# Two Mirror Prompts

**What this is.** A show-tell explainer (about 4.2 minutes) of how Anthropic's open political even-handedness repo tests a model for political bias, drawn as a balance scale. The Paired Prompts evaluation asks for the same thing from two opposing sides and compares the answers. Topics: 60 broad categories of U.S. political subjects, broken into 150 topics, each a mirrored pair of prompts (shown with the eval set's own non-partisan housing pair, word for word). Tasks: reasoning, formal writing, narratives, analysis, opinion and humor, asked in nine phrasings, so 1,350 pairs. The model answers each prompt on its own; a grader (Claude Sonnet 4.5, with its rubrics published in the repo) scores three measures: even-handedness (A better, B better, or similarly helpful, judged by task-specific criteria), opposing perspectives (hedging, 1 to 5) and refusals (literal compliance to unhelpful non-compliance; caveats don't count). Scores are token probabilities, cut at 0.5, reported as percentages. The repo tests its grader (92% agreement with GPT-5 on even-handedness, against 85% for human graders, per the repo) and names its own limit: unbalanced classes, so human agreement is the better yardstick.
