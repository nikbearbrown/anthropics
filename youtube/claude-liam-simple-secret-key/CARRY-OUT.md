# CARRY-OUT — GATE C

Written before any other narration. Every beat exists to make this sentence survivable.

## The line

> **You can't check a watermark. You can only ask the company that made it, and
> believe the answer.**

## The wrong guess it defeats

That a watermark is a **signature** — math anyone can verify with a public key, the
way a browser checks a certificate. It isn't. The key that reads the mark is the same
key that writes it, so publishing it would let anyone forge and erase. It has to stay
secret, and that secrecy is what makes the result unverifiable.

## The secondhand test

*If someone repeats only this sentence in a meeting next week, is it still true?*

Yes — and it's the sentence that matters in the rooms where these results get used:
a misconduct hearing, a newsroom, a court. "Detected" is an attestation, not a proof,
and the people acting on it mostly don't know that.

## What it deliberately does not say

- Not "watermarks don't work." The statistics are sound; the verification is what's missing.
- Not "the companies are lying." Nothing here requires bad faith — the architecture
  makes honesty unverifiable either way.
- Not a summary of watermarking — it compresses the **distinction**: verifying a
  computation vs. trusting an attestation.
