# CARRY-OUT — GATE C

Written before any other narration. Every beat exists to make this sentence survivable.

## The line

> **You don't need bad faith. You just need the same company to build the detector
> and pay for it working.**

## The wrong guess it defeats

That this is a story about a company **hiding** something — that the stated reasons
were a cover, and the fix is to catch them at it.

It isn't, and that framing is weaker in every direction. The stated problems are
real: paraphrase does defeat these marks, and false positives do fall hardest on
people writing in a second language. The argument doesn't need anyone to be lying.
It needs only the incentive to exist — and it does, for every provider, permanently.

## The secondhand test

*If someone repeats only this sentence in a meeting next week, is it still true?*

Yes, and it can't be rebutted by a denial — which is exactly why it's the right
sentence. "We're acting in good faith" is compatible with it.

## What it deliberately does not say

- Not "they lied about why." The reel never asserts a motive it can't evidence.
- Not "detection is being suppressed." It says the incentive runs one way and that
  from outside you can't distinguish the explanations.
- Not a summary of the case — it compresses the **distinction**: motive vs. structure.
