## Deliberately not claimed

This is the reel's most important section. It makes claims adjacent to a named
company's motives, so the line is drawn explicitly:

- **No motive is asserted.** The script never says a detector was withheld *because*
  of revenue. It says the incentive exists and that outsiders cannot distinguish the
  explanations. S15/S16 make that symmetry the point rather than a hedge.
- **The reported numbers are marked as reported.** The accuracy figure and the
  user-attrition figure come from press accounts of internal documents. S11 says so
  in the narration, not in a footnote — and S12 removes the argument's dependence on
  them, so the reel survives if a number is wrong.
- **The stated objections are granted, not sneered at.** Paraphrase evasion and
  false positives on second-language writers are treated as real, because they are.
  Granting them is what makes the structural claim land.
- **No policy verdict.** The printer comparison explains why a uniform requirement
  removes the first-mover penalty. The reel stops there and does not argue for a law.

## Handoff prompt (BHTF, read aloud then discussed)

> "Find a case where the party required to measure a problem is the party the
> measurement costs — emissions self-reporting, safety incident logs, a platform's
> own moderation numbers. Write down who would lose if the measurement got sharper,
> and what would have to be true for them to sharpen it anyway."

Why it's worth running: the pattern isn't about AI. Once you can name it, you start
seeing it in places where nobody has ever suggested wrongdoing — which is the point.
