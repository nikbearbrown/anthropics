# CARRY-OUT — GATE C

Written before any other narration. Every beat exists to make this sentence survivable.

## The line

> **Nobody passed a law requiring this everywhere. A global setting is a company's
> choice, not a government's order.**

## The wrong guess it defeats

The Brussels-effect story: *the EU forced this on everyone.* It didn't. The EU rule
bites when a system is placed on the EU market or its output is used in the Union —
not on every file generated anywhere on earth. California's rule turns on a provider's
size and its accessibility in California. Neither says "mark everything, everywhere."

## The secondhand test

*If someone repeats only this sentence in a meeting next week, is it still true?*

Yes, and it survives the follow-up that usually kills these sentences — "so which law
was it?" The answer is that the question has a jurisdiction attached, always.

## What it deliberately does not say

- Not "the rules don't matter." They bind, they carry real penalties, and several are
  already live.
- Not "companies did this to look good." No motive is asserted; the reel flags the
  cheap-pipeline explanation as an inference, not a finding.
- Not a survey of AI law — it compresses the **distinction**: product setting vs.
  legal mandate.
