## Deliberately not claimed

- **No geofence claim.** The reel never says California requires marking only for
  Californians — the statute's operative command isn't textually limited that way, and
  asserting otherwise would be wrong in a checkable direction.
- **No "identical deadline."** Incumbent EU systems got a transition into December, so
  the two dates are not the same hard technical deadline.
- **No motive.** S12 flags the cheap-pipeline reason as a guess. The reel says the
  choice was a company's; it never claims to know why the company made it.
- **No survey.** Six jurisdictions are alluded to structurally (S09–S10) and none is
  enumerated. Listing them would be the unreadable direction the register exists to avoid.

## Handoff prompt (BHTF, read aloud then discussed)

> "Pick one thing your tools do automatically that you assume a law requires — a
> cookie banner, a data-retention window, an age gate, a disclosure line. Find the
> actual rule, then check who it binds, what it covers, and where it applies. Note
> every place the product does more than the rule asks."

Why it's worth running: the gap between what a product does and what a law demands is
almost always occupied by someone's choice — and choices can be argued with.
