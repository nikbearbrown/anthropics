# NARRATION — what-is-behind-the-model

## B00 (cold open)
Konnichiwa, Bear. Computational skepticism is the lens, and one line under this whole playlist: the model's confidence is a property of the model, not a property of the world. Everything else here is downstream of that. Here's why it does what it does.

## B01 (wrong model)
The wrong model: reading model behavior as personality or mood. It's being lazy today. It's gotten worse recently. It's in a bad state. These are human explanations for a statistical process.

## B02 (right model)
The right model: context is a physical constraint. What the model can see, what it has lost, what the answer changed. Starved context produces pattern completion, not knowledge. The variable is what you gave it.

## B03 (what's in the playlist)
Four things: context and why the model forgets, why it invents when starved of information, how hedging and refusals work mechanically, and what it actually costs to make it think longer.

## B04 (do now)
Next time it's wrong, ask it what it actually had in context before it answered.

## B05 (verdict)
The playlist that makes the other nine cheaper to learn. Watch it second, after basics. Falsifiable line: if knowing the mechanism behind a behavior produces no improvement in how you set up the context, the mechanical explanation is academically interesting and practically useless.
