## A Note about AI

College success is the textbook category where the model's strengths and the field's content most overlap — and where the overlap is most likely to mislead the student. The book teaches study skills, time management, communication, and self-directed learning. The model is fluent in all four. The question the note addresses is what happens to those skills when the model is in the workflow that the skills were meant to govern.

The model can produce a study schedule, a note-taking template, a structured outline of any reading, a draft of any essay, a summary of any chapter, a flashcard set for any topic, and a personalized advice paragraph for any situation a student describes. The production is fast, professional-looking, and easy to mistake for the work the chapter is asking the student to do. The misidentification is the failure mode this note exists to name.

Where the model genuinely helps: explaining the underlying principles of study techniques (why spaced repetition works, why active recall beats rereading, why interleaving improves transfer), producing the canonical research on a specific habit (sleep and learning, the testing effect, deliberate practice), generating practice questions over material you have already engaged with, surfacing the structural decisions in an assignment (what is being asked, what counts as evidence, what the rubric weights are), and producing alternative framings of a confusing concept until one lands. The model is also useful as a sparring partner against your own rationalizations — paste your reasons for not studying tonight and ask the model to mount the strongest objection.

Where the model does damage: producing the work the chapter asks you to do. A model-drafted study plan is not your study plan; a model-summarized reading is not your reading; a model-written reflection is not your reflection. The chapter exists because the act of producing each of these is what builds the underlying skill. If the model produces them for you, you have lost the curricular value of the assignment and you have nothing to be reflective about. The model will also produce confident personalized advice based on a brief description of your situation — advice that will sound right and will not be tuned to facts the model cannot have.

A specific failure mode worth naming: the model is particularly fluent in the *meta* of college success — talking about studying, time management, motivation — in ways that can substitute for actually doing them. A student who spends an hour discussing their study habits with the model and ten minutes studying has had a productive-feeling hour that is the opposite of productive.

The rule that covers all three: principles from the model; the doing from you. The skills this book teaches are skills only the doing builds. The model can explain what to do, surface what you are avoiding, and pressure-test your plan. It cannot do the studying, take the notes, write the reflection, or have the conversation with your professor on your behalf. The model can talk about college success competently. Doing it is your job.
