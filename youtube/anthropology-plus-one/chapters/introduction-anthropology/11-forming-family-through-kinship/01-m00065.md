Whom do you consider part of your family? How many mothers do you have?
Could you or would you marry your cousin? Each of these questions asks
us to consider how our societies structure kinship. Families reflect the
social and cultural contexts in which they are formed. Through the study
of kinship within our own and other societies, we better understand such
things as the connections that individuals have across generations; how
a cultural group manages procreation and childcare; the ways that
material assets, power, and influence are inherited; and the choices an
individual has for marriage.
