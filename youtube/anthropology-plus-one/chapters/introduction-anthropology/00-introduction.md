## A Note about AI

Anthropology is the academic discipline most explicitly aware of its own colonial history, most contested in its contemporary self-understanding, and most fluently misrepresented by sources that aggregate across many anthropological positions. The model is exactly such a source. The note examines what that means for using the model as a study aid.

The model has read major anthropological texts, ethnographies, theoretical syntheses, and the contemporary literature on decolonizing the discipline. It will produce a competent paragraph on participant observation, structural-functionalism, the reflexive turn, the anthropology of any specific topic, or any of the major theorists in the canon. The fluency is real. It becomes a problem when the question shifts from textbook description to anthropological judgment — because judgment in anthropology requires attention to the position of the observer, the politics of representation, and the contested claims of insider authority, and the model has flattened all three.

Where the model genuinely helps: explaining theoretical frameworks (what structuralism is, what practice theory means, what the ontological turn proposes), producing the canonical readings of major ethnographies, surveying the major debates the field has worked through (the reflexivity debates of the 1980s, the gender debates of the 1970s and beyond, the contemporary debates about indigenous self-representation), and producing structured comparisons across schools and methods.

Where the model does damage: characterizing specific cultures, peoples, or practices in language that subtly carries the inflection of the colonial sources that the discipline has been trying to read past. The model has trained on enormous quantities of writing about non-Western societies, much of it produced from outside those societies. The averages the model reproduces are not neutral. Ask the model about a specific cultural practice and you will get a description that may flatten the practice's internal complexity, treat contested anthropological interpretations as settled, or omit the perspective of insiders who have written about themselves.

A specific failure mode worth naming: the model produces confident generalizations about cultural groups when the working anthropological literature on those groups is heterogeneous, debated, and increasingly written by members of the groups themselves. The generalizations sound like ethnography. They are aggregates of ethnography, which is a different thing.

The rule that covers all three: theoretical frameworks from the model; cultural specifics from ethnographies named in your syllabus and increasingly from voices inside the cultures being described. The discipline's contemporary practice is to attend to who is speaking. The model has no who. Read its prose with that absence in mind.
