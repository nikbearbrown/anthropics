The *α* bromination of carbonyl compounds by Br2 in acetic acid is
limited to aldehydes and ketones because acids, esters, and amides don't
enolize to a sufficient extent. Carboxylic acids, however, can be *α*
brominated by a mixture of Br2 and PBr3 in the *Hell--Volhard--Zelinskii
(HVZ) reaction.*

</figure>

The Hell--Volhard--Zelinskii reaction is a bit more complex than it
looks and actually involves *α* substitution of an acid bromide enol
rather than a carboxylic acid enol. The process begins by reaction of
the carboxylic acid with PBr3 to form an acid bromide plus HBr
([\#](#)). The HBr then catalyzes enolization of the acid bromide, and
the resultant enol reacts with Br2 in an *α*-substitution reaction to
give an *α*-bromo acid bromide. Addition of water hydrolyzes the acid
bromide in a nucleophilic acyl substitution reaction and yields the
*α*-bromo carboxylic acid product.
