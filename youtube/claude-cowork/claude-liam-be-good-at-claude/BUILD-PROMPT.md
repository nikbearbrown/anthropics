# BUILD PROMPT — be-good-at-claude

## Episode concept
The source article is written in all lowercase — matter-of-fact, zero pretension, ruthlessly practical. It describes six levels of Claude mastery as if explaining a video game to someone who has never played: you are already on a level, here is the next one, go. The reel preserves that register. Narration is casual and direct, sentences are short, jargon is explained immediately. The through-line is the AskUserQuestion trick — it appears at level 1 and returns at the final boss. Everything else builds on it.

## Beat-by-beat production notes

**B01 — COMPOSER_ASK (cold open)**
Zdravo (Bosnian/Serbian/Croatian greeting). Lowercase-adjacent narration from the start. The ask is casual: "how do I actually get good at using Claude?" Liam badge bottom-left.

**B02 — SPARK: Claude asks you first. (Level 1)**
The AskUserQuestion trick in a single card. Narration explains the mechanic and lands the punchline: that one line makes you better than most "prompt engineers."

**B03 — SPARK: Talk. Don't type. (Level 2)**
Short card. Level 2 = voice input. Wispr Flow named as an example tool with free tier. Note: narration also plants level 3 (download app, Cowork tab, make folder, drag it in) in the same beat to keep pace — two levels in one beat is intentional here given the simplicity of level 2's concept.

**B04 — SPARK: Real files. Your folder. (Level 3)**
Cowork distinction from browser version. Key message: Cowork makes the actual file. Narration delivers the demo example (spreadsheet prompt) inline.

**B05 — SPARK: Connect everything. (Level 4)**
App connectors. Navigation path is explicit: Customize → Connectors. Three examples: Gmail, Calendar, meeting notes. Narration delivers the "context is everything" philosophy in one line.

**B06 — SPARK: /skill-creator exists. (Level 5)**
Skills as reusable playbooks. The meta-joke (Claude has a skill to make skills) is preserved. Narration gives the exact prompt pattern.

**B07 — ILLUSTRATION: Claude Code build flow (Final boss)**
This is the one multi-step concept in the reel — it needs an illustration. Four-step horizontal flow: prompt → AskUserQuestion panel → building → live page. Flat design, terracotta arrows, no screenshots. Narration walks through the booking-page example.

**B08 — HANDOFF**
Minimal. Pick your level. Do the next one. Tonight. Narration is three short lines to match the source's urgency.

**B09 — OUTRO**
Closing note: most people never try level 1. The bar is just trying. Episode title "SIX LEVELS" in caps. Liam sign-off.
