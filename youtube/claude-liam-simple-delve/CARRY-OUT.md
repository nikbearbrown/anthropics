# CARRY-OUT — GATE C

Written before any other narration. Every beat exists to make this sentence survivable.

## The line

> **A word that shows up again and again is a habit, not a watermark. The watermark
> never picks the same favourite twice.**

## The wrong guess it defeats

That a conspicuous AI-sounding word — *delve*, *underscore*, *intricate* — is the
watermark surfacing. It's the opposite: repetition across unrelated documents is
evidence *against* watermarking, because a keyed watermark redraws its preferences at
every position. A mark that kept favouring the same words would be strippable with a
find-and-replace, which is why none of the common ones do that.

## The secondhand test

*If someone repeats only this sentence in a meeting next week, is it still true?*

Yes — and it inverts the listener's instinct rather than adding to it, which is the
kind of sentence that survives retelling because it's surprising enough to be worth
repeating accurately.

## What it deliberately does not say

- Not "AI tells aren't real." They're measurable at corpus scale, and the reel says so.
- Not "watermarks never favour fixed words." Some schemes deliberately do — flagged at S12.
- Not a taxonomy of stylometry — it compresses the **distinction**: a habit lives in
  the weights, a watermark lives in a per-position coin flip.
