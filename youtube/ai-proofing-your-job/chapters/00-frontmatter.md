## Preface

This book exists because the standard advice for mid-career professionals facing AI disruption is wrong in a specific, damaging way. The advice says: learn to use the tools. The implication is that the people who learn to prompt AI fastest will win. This book argues the opposite. The professionals who will thrive are not the ones who delegate the most — they are the ones who know exactly what not to delegate.

The urgency is practical. AI tools are genuinely impressive, and the pressure to adopt them is real. But frequent AI use without a framework for what to protect erodes the judgment it was supposed to augment. A management consultant using AI on tasks outside the jagged frontier performs 19% worse than an unassisted peer on the same tasks. The output looks better. The thinking is weaker. This is the deskilling trap, and it is already happening to professionals who believe they are getting more productive.

This book gives mid-career domain experts the map and the method: a seven-tier taxonomy of cognitive labor, grounded in labor market evidence, applied through six industry-specific case studies. By the time you finish it, you should be able to name the three to five things in your specific work that AI cannot replace — and restructure your daily workflow to protect them.
