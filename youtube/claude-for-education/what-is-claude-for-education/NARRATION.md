Sawubona, Bear. This channel teaches through computational skepticism — and skepticism here isn't a personality, it's a method. A set of moves you can perform on a claim, that can be taught, and that someone can watch you do. Which is exactly why it belongs in a classroom. ## B01 (wrong model)
The wrong model: the ban-it-or-embrace-it fight. A policy argument standing in for a pedagogy argument. Neither side is asking which skill the assignment is protecting.

## B02 (right model)
The right model: the line between scaffold and crutch, drawn per assignment — because it moves depending on what the assignment is protecting. Name the skill first. Then decide where AI goes.

## B03 (what's in the playlist)
Four things: naming the skill an assignment actually protects, what to hand over to AI, designing work that survives AI, and being straight with students instead of policing them.

## B04 (do now)
Pick one assignment and write down the single skill it exists to build.

## B05 (verdict)
For teachers, and for students who want to stay good at things. This playlist never teaches anyone to get around a ban or an integrity policy — say that clearly. Falsifiable line: if students who use AI as a scaffold on this playlist's model outperform students who don't on the underlying skill, the scaffold-versus-crutch line is drawn in the right place.
