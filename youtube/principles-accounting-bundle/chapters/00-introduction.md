## A Note about AI

Accounting is the field where the model handles the structural vocabulary well and the application poorly, and the application is the curriculum. The note examines where the model belongs in a student's workflow.

The model has read every introductory accounting textbook in print. It will explain the accounting equation, the double-entry system, the four financial statements, the accounting cycle, and the major principles (GAAP, conservatism, matching, going concern). The fluency is useful for vocabulary and orientation. It becomes a problem when the work shifts to specific journal entries, specific reconciliations, or specific computations on real numbers — because the model is not a careful enough calculator to be trusted with the work, and the work is the curriculum.

Where the model genuinely helps: explaining principles (why we accrue revenue when earned rather than when received, what depreciation is conceptually doing, what the difference between operating and capital leases meant before lease accounting changed and what changed), walking through worked examples at the textbook's level of detail, producing structured comparisons across accounting treatments (LIFO vs. FIFO, percentage-of-completion vs. completed-contract), and generating practice problems with answer keys at graded difficulty.

Where the model does damage: producing specific journal entries for transactions with real numbers, computing depreciation schedules, preparing trial balances. The model will produce entries that look correct and contain a wrong account, a wrong amount, or a missed adjusting entry. The errors are exactly the errors students make, which means the model cannot reliably catch them.

A specific failure mode worth naming: the model is unreliable on current GAAP. Standards update; the model has read both the current standards and the older ones; it does not always know which is current. A specific accounting question about revenue recognition, lease accounting, or financial instruments may receive a confident answer based on the standard that was current at training time, not the standard current now.

The rule that covers all three: principles from the model; the actual accounting from your work, the textbook's worked solutions, and current FASB or IFRS guidance for any specific question. Use the model to understand why a treatment is what it is. Do the journal entries yourself.
