# Feedback — Priya S., median vs mean

## Mechanism
The housing sentence carries the real mechanism: the mansion drags the mean upward while the median stays put because half the houses are still on the cheaper side. The five-number list in the middle does not do the same work — no value in 1, 6, 6, 7, 8 is named as the outlier, so the reader has to guess why the two summaries differ. Name the outlier in the numeric example the way you named the mansion, and the mechanism will land twice instead of once.

## Example
The mansion-in-the-neighborhood case is concrete, plausible, and directly serves the claim — this is the strongest move in the paragraph. The numeric list beside it is doing example-work too, which doubles the illustration without deepening it. Consider cutting the list, or reworking it so the two examples show different facets rather than the same one.

## Argument
"Median is better because it is not affected as much as the average" is functioning as a controlling sentence, and the paragraph does stay loyal to it. The wording is soft, though — "better" without a domain ("better for skewed data," "better when a few extreme values are present") lets the claim float. A sharper controlling sentence would give the rest of the paragraph something firmer to defend.

## Precision
Two vocabulary issues drag on the precision score. First, the piece uses "average" to mean the arithmetic mean; in a paragraph whose whole point is distinguishing summary statistics, those two words need to stay separated. Second, the key technical phrase — resistant to outliers — never appears, so the mechanism is described only in everyday language.

## Growth
Rewrite the opening sentence to state plainly that the median is resistant to outliers while the mean is not, then let the mansion example do exactly that work — the rest of the paragraph will tighten around it.
